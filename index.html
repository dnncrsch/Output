Celebrity or Influencer?

What CBA students trust, and what brands should do about it.

About the study

100 students, one question

A quantitative survey of 100 College of Business Administration students at Tayabas Western Academy, comparing celebrity endorsement and influencer marketing on brand engagement and consumer trust. Grounded in Source Credibility Theory (Hovland & Weiss, 1951; Ohanian, 1990).

What we found

Both work. Influencers earn a little more trust.

Ratings on a 4-point scale. Both strategies landed in the “moderately influenced” range.

Brand engagement: a tie

2.80 vs 2.78

Celebrity vs. influencer. No significant difference (p = 0.866).

Consumer trust: influencers lead

2.85 vs 2.92

Significant, but a modest edge (p = 0.0096).

Age and gender

Within every age and gender group, neither strategy significantly outperformed the other.

n = 100 CBA students, mostly female and aged 19–22. Pilot with your own audience first.

Two tools, two jobs

Fame for reach. Relatability for trust.

Celebrity

Fame from TV, film, music, sports.

Aspirational, reaches everyone.

Best for visibility.

Influencer

Fame built on TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram.

Relatable, niche communities.

Best for trust.

Where respondents scroll daily

YouTube was used weekly by 42%, which suits longer reviews. Give X little budget for this audience.

The weak spot
People watch and like, but rarely comment (2.34–2.56), share, or follow.
The playbook

Five moves for your next campaign

1. Split the jobs

Celebrities for attention and awareness. Influencers for reviews, demos, and trust.

2. Tilt toward influencers for trust

Shift part of the budget, not all. Choose credible, on-topic creators over big follower counts.

3. Get viewers to join in

Add polls, Q&As, comment-to-win, and challenges. Reply to comments.

4. Stay authentic

Disclose sponsorships, keep content honest, favor long-term partners.

5. Go where they scroll

Lead with TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram. Track comments, shares, follows, and inquiries.

Before you hire an endorser

Ask these five questions

Tap a question to tick it off.

  • 1
    Fit. Does this person naturally match our product?
  • 2
    Honesty. Do they give real reviews, not only praise?
  • 3
    Load. How many brands do they already promote?
  • 4
    Action. Does the post invite people to comment, share, or follow?
  • 5
    Proof. How will we measure results beyond likes?
